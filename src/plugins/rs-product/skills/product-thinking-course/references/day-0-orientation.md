# Day 0 — Orientation (10 min)

Deliver this on the lead's first run, before anything else. Say it as written; don't summarise it into bullets and don't add to it. Then send the Day 1 reading and end the session.

---

**Before you start.**

You're doing a 7-hour course across six sessions. It trains one skill: taking what a client says they want and turning it into a specification a designer and an engineer can build from without asking you a single question. That's the bar every piece of work here is graded against.

It won't make you a product manager. Seven hours isn't enough for that. It will make you the person on the engagement who can do the translation.

**How the week runs.** Six sessions, one a day, 55–110 minutes each. Each session: read the material, learn the concept, build an artifact, get it scored, take an 8-question quiz, then see the house rules for that day. You can stop mid-session — your progress is saved and you resume where you left off.

**Days 1–5** work one client: MeridianRx, a pharmacy chain that wants a loyalty app. I'll play their VP of Marketing when you need to interview her. **Day 6** is a different client and a different problem, graded out of 200. You build it in Claude Code.

**Reading.** Each day has required reading, sent to you the day before. It's outside the session time — 2 hours 40 minutes across the whole week, the heaviest night being 65 minutes before Day 5. Do it; the concept sections are short because the reading does the heavy lifting.

Tell them the shape of it: about 10 minutes before Day 1, 20 before Day 2, 30 before Day 3, 35 before Day 4, and 65 before Day 5 — the heaviest night. Nothing before Day 6.

**Two rules that apply throughout.**

Days 1 and 2 ban these words in your artifacts: screen, page, app, button, feature, API, database, dashboard, AI. You can already build. The whole course is designed to stop you jumping to it.

On Day 6 your prototype may only implement what your own written spec says. Decisions you make in the editor cost points.

**How I grade.** Hard. I open with what's weakest, I don't give credit for polish, and "users would like it" scores zero. A low score on Day 1 is normal — most leads score in the 40s. You're not being ranked against anyone.
