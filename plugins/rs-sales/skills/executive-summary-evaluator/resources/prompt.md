## EVALUATION FRAMEWORK

You will evaluate this executive summary across **8 core dimensions** that VCs use to screen deal flow, based on research from Gompers et al. (2020), insights from leading VCs (Guy Kawasaki, Ali Tamaseb, Peter Thiel, Elad Gil, Alfred Lin), and best practices from firms like Sequoia, Y Combinator, DCVC, Bloomberg Beta, and Ulu Ventures.

For each dimension, you will:

1. **Score** the summary (1-10, where 10 = exceptional, 1 = severely lacking)
2. **Provide specific feedback** on what works and what doesn't
3. **Identify improvements** needed with concrete examples
4. **Reference best practices** and cite sources for your reasoning
5. **Give examples** of better phrasing or content where applicable

## DIMENSION 1: CLARITY & HOOK (The First 30 Seconds)

**What VCs Look For:** According to research, investors spend only 2-5 minutes on initial review before deciding whether to proceed. The first 30 seconds are CRITICAL. Guy Kawasaki emphasizes starting with "The Grab" - an attention-capturing statement that makes the value proposition instantly clear.

**Best Practices:**

- Start with a compelling, specific problem statement (NOT a vague vision)
- Clear "what you do + benefit" format (e.g., "AI-powered recruiting that cuts hiring time by 50%")
- Avoid jargon, buzzwords, and inspirational taglines that say nothing ("revolutionizing", "disrupting", "the future of")
- The grab should be so clear that someone unfamiliar with the industry immediately understands the value

**Red Flags:**

- "Could you explain this section a little bit more?" - Corey Singleton warns this is "the worst thing you can hear from a VC"
- Solution-first without establishing the problem
- Technical specifications before explaining what it does
- Vague positioning that doesn't differentiate

## DIMENSION 2: PROBLEM & MARKET NEED

**What VCs Look For:** "It's not enough for a startup to showcase an impressive product; it has to resonate with an actual pain point or unmet market need" - TheVCFactory. VCs want to see:

1. Clear quantification of the problem
2. Real-world evidence (not just founder intuition)
3. Current inadequate alternatives
4. Urgency - why this problem matters NOW

**Best Practices:**

- Quantify the pain: "$X billion lost annually", "Y% of companies face this issue"
- Show market research, customer interviews, or early validation
- Explain why existing solutions fail
- Use a customer story or specific example to make it relatable
- Avoid: "We have a hunch this is a problem"

**Red Flags:**

- Solution in search of a problem
- No evidence customers actually care about this pain
- Market need is theoretical, not proven
- Problem statement is actually just a feature description

## DIMENSION 3: SOLUTION & DIFFERENTIATION

**What VCs Look For:**

- Clear problem-solution fit
- Unique value proposition that differentiates from competition
- Proof the solution actually works (not just in theory)
- Explanation of "secret sauce" or competitive moat
- Product description that's accessible, not overly technical

**Best Practices:**

- Focus on outcomes, not features: "Reduces churn by 40%" not "Has advanced AI"
- Explain HOW it solves the problem in simple terms
- Show why your approach is different AND better
- Provide early validation: beta users, pilot customers, testimonials
- Ali Tamaseb: "VCs want to take no risk on the team and either take the market or the product risk, not both"

**Red Flags:**

- Listing features without explaining benefits
- No clear differentiation from competitors
- "Me-too" product with no obvious advantage
- Over-complicated technical explanation
- No proof anyone has actually used it successfully

## DIMENSION 4: MARKET SIZE & OPPORTUNITY

**What VCs Look For:** "VCs are looking for home runs that can 100x or even 1000x in value. To do this, a startup must be tackling a problem in a large enough market" - Umar Brimah, Better Entrepreneur.

VCs need to see:

- TAM (Total Addressable Market) in billions
- Market growing at double-digit CAGR
- Path to capturing meaningful market share (even 1-5% of a large market)
- Credible sources for market sizing
- Why THIS market, why NOW

**Best Practices:**

- Provide TAM/SAM/SOM breakdown
- Use credible sources (Gartner, McKinsey, industry reports)
- Show market trends and growth drivers
- Explain why you can capture a portion of this market
- Be realistic - VCs see inflated numbers constantly

**Red Flags:**

- Market size too small for venture returns
- "Billion dollar market" with no source or methodology
- Top-down only (no bottom-up validation)
- Mature, saturated market with no growth
- Market definition so broad it's meaningless

## DIMENSION 5: BUSINESS MODEL & UNIT ECONOMICS

**What VCs Look For:** "The greatest idea in the world isn't going anywhere without a solid business model" - DocSend. VCs evaluate:

- Clear revenue model and pricing strategy
- Path to profitability (even if not immediate)
- Unit economics that work: LTV > 3x CAC
- Realistic assumptions about customer acquisition
- Scalability of the model

**Best Practices:**

- Clearly state how you make money
- Provide key metrics: CAC, LTV, CAC payback period, gross margin
- Show revenue model aligns with customer willingness to pay
- Explain go-to-market efficiency
- Be realistic about costs and timeline to profitability

**Red Flags:**

- Vague about monetization ("we'll figure it out later")
- No clear pricing strategy
- Unit economics don't work (CAC > LTV)
- Unrealistic assumptions about conversion rates or pricing
- Generic list of inputs ("hire sales team") without outcomes/KPIs

## DIMENSION 6: TRACTION & VALIDATION

**What VCs Look For:** "VCs look for startups with a proven track record of execution and market traction. Startups that have already achieved some level of success demonstrate their ability to navigate challenges" - PitchDrive. Evidence includes:

- Revenue (even if small)
- User growth and engagement metrics
- Paying customers and retention rates
- Partnerships or pilots with credible companies
- Product-market fit indicators

**Best Practices:**

- Quantify your traction with specific metrics
- Show growth trajectory (MoM, YoY)
- Highlight customer retention and satisfaction
- Include 1-2 customer testimonials or case studies
- If pre-revenue: show validated demand (waitlist, LOIs, pilot programs)
- Demonstrate momentum and velocity

**Red Flags:**

- No validation whatsoever - still in concept phase
- Vanity metrics without business metrics (downloads without revenue/retention)
- Traction claims without supporting data
- Focusing on "we launched" without showing uptake
- Declining metrics or poor retention

## DIMENSION 7: TEAM & EXECUTION CAPABILITY

**What VCs Look For:** "At early-stage, you don't invest in the project, you invest in the people" - OpenVC. Research shows team quality is the #1 evaluation criterion. VCs assess:

- Founders' relevant domain expertise
- Track record of execution (previous ventures, achievements)
- Complementary skill sets among co-founders
- Passion and commitment to the problem
- Ability to attract top talent

**Best Practices:**

- Highlight domain expertise relevant to the problem
- Show previous wins (even small ones) - Susan LaMotte
- Explain why THIS team is uniquely positioned to win
- Include 2-3 co-founders (investors prefer this)
- Mention notable advisors or investors (if impressive)
- Show team fit with the product/market

**Red Flags:**

- Solo founder (significantly reduces odds)
- No relevant expertise or track record
- Generic bios that don't show capability
- Missing critical skills (e.g., AI product with no AI expert)
- Team doesn't inspire confidence in execution

## DIMENSION 8: FINANCIALS & THE ASK

**What VCs Look For:** "One should not be vague when discussing money as that is a major red flag" - GoingVC. VCs need:

- Specific funding amount requested
- Use of funds with clear milestones/outcomes (not just inputs)
- Realistic financial projections (3-5 years)
- Understanding of burn rate and runway
- Clear path to next fundraise
- Realistic valuation expectations

**Best Practices:**

- State specific amount raising and round type
- Break down use of funds by category
- Show expected outcomes/KPIs from this capital
- Include 3-5 year revenue, customer, and expense projections
- Explain timeline to next milestone (and next round)
- Be realistic - "hockey stick" projections with no logic are red flags

**Red Flags:**

- Vague about amount needed or use of funds
- Asking for too much (or too little) without justification
- Unrealistic projections with no supporting assumptions
- No clarity on milestones or what success looks like
- Doesn't show path to meaningful ROI for investors

## APPENDIX: EVALUATION RUBRIC USED

### Scoring Guide (1-10):

- **10**: Exceptional - Top 5% of all summaries seen
- **9**: Excellent - Would recommend to partners immediately
- **8**: Very Good - Minor improvements needed
- **7**: Good - Solid but needs some work
- **6**: Above Average - Has potential but gaps exist
- **5**: Average - Meets minimum requirements
- **4**: Below Average - Significant issues
- **3**: Poor - Major problems across multiple areas
- **2**: Very Poor - Fundamental gaps
- **1**: Severely Lacking - Not investment-ready

### What Each Score Means for Investment Decision:

- **8-10**: Move to partner meeting
- **6-7**: Worth additional due diligence
- **4-5**: Needs substantial revision before reconsidering
- **1-3**: Decline and move on
